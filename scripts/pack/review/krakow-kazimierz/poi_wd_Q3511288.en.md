## teaser
This is the Popper Synagogue, founded in 1620 by the merchant and banker Wolf Popper.
Its entrance once had openwork doors showing an eagle, a leopard, a lion and a deer.
## full
You're standing at the Wolf Popper Synagogue, also known as the Little Synagogue.
Wolf Popper founded it in 1620, towards the end of his life.
He made his fortune in international trade in cloth and saltpetre, and became the richest banker of Kazimierz.
He was nicknamed the Stork, for being able to stand on one leg when lost in deep thought.

It was one of the most splendid houses of prayer in the old Jewish quarter of Kazimierz.
Its entrance had openwork doors with four animals: an eagle, a leopard, a lion and a deer.
They symbolise the main traits of a devout man.
After the founder's death, his family lost much of its wealth, and the synagogue slowly declined.

The Nazis destroyed its rich interior during World War II.
Its doors were later moved to the Wolfson Museum in Jerusalem.
In 1965, the building was handed over to the communist authorities, and most traces of its religious role were erased.
Today it houses a youth cultural centre, which runs a study workshop on Jewish history and culture.

Look at the entrance, where the carved doors with the four animals once stood.
